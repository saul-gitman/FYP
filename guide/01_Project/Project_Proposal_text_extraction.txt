To design and fabricate asymmetric aerogel interleaves using freeze-casting 
techniques for structural integration into CFRP laminates via resin infusion. 
• 
To conduct a comprehensive engineering analysis of the multifunctional 
properties, specifically quantifying through-thickness thermal conductivity, 
solar reflectance, and interlaminar shear strength. 
• 
To prototype a representative thermo-optically functional CFRP component

conditions, comparing its heat dissipation and solar-shielding effectiveness 
against standard CFRP benchmarks.

Electrified aviation and UAVs require multifunctional airframe materials 
capable of managing internal heat from electronics while resisting external 
solar radiation. While carbon fibre reinforced polymer (CFRP) composites 
offer superior mechanical properties, their poor through-thickness thermal 
conductivity leads to heat accumulation. This project involves the 
engineering design and prototyping of a structurally integrated interleave 
to provide asymmetric thermo-optical regulation within CFRP laminates. 
The manufacturing phase focuses on creating a dual-layered porous 
architecture using freeze-casting. The design features a carbon-rich inner 
domain to redistribute internal heat and a boron-nitride-rich outer domain 
to reflect solar radiation. These interleaves will be integrated into CFRP via 
vacuum assisted resin infusion (VARI). A key engineering challenge is 
ensuring the resin infusion process preserves the porosity of aerogels while 
maintaining structural integrity. To validate these models, engineering 
testing will be performed for thermal transport, solar reflectance, and 
infrared thermography to monitor real-time heat dissipation. 
The outcome will be a validated framework for thermo-optically functional 
CFRPs. By replacing heavy active cooling systems with lightweight, 
passive interleaves, this project provides a scalable solution to enhance the 
thermal reliability and efficiency of next-generation electrified mobility 
platforms.
