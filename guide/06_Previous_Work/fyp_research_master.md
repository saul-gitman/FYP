## Part D — The experimental plan as expressed in our conversations (NOT copied from the uploaded Word document)

**Research aim:** Design a lightweight asymmetric CFRP panel that can **limit absorption of external solar irradiation** while **spreading/distributing internally generated heat**, then validate the combined thermal, optical and mechanical response. Passive cooling and efficient conduction are not the same thing; a single scalar k does not establish a one-way heat valve.

1. **Define research question.** Compare baseline CFRP with an asymmetric CFRP laminate to explore how to reduce external solar heating while improving heat spreading of internal heat sources. Do not claim thermal diode behavior without a directional heat-flow experiment.
2. **Define composition controls.** Consider BN-to-carbon-rich filler ratio (BN/C), filler/solids concentration, and separate BN-rich exterior vs carbon-rich interior. Carbon type is not finally fixed in the earlier user-specified variables: graphene/CNT are candidate systems; do not equate C with CNF.
3. **Define freeze-casting controls.** Vary freezing temperature and freezing duration, which the user explicitly named. Evaluate freezing direction/thermal gradient, freezing-front velocity or cooling rate, suspension dispersion, and drying as additional literature-derived candidates rather than confirmed fixed settings.
4. **Prepare dispersions and aligned porous structures.** Disperse chosen BN and carbon-family fillers in an appropriate binder/solvent, cast and directionally freeze with controlled thermal boundary, then remove ice by freeze-drying. Determine detailed dispersion, binder and freezing protocol from cited papers and pilot trials, not the uploaded Word recipe.
5. **Characterize resulting morphology.** Check aligned cells/channels, possible honeycomb/hexagonal cross-sections (hypothesis, not guaranteed), pore shape/width, wall alignment, connectivity, porosity, bulk density, shrinkage and thickness; use micrographs and structure measurements.
6. **Integrate CFRP laminate.** Build a carbon-rich inner interleave for heat distribution and a BN-rich outer-facing porous region for sunlight reflection; combine with carbon-fiber layers and consider vacuum-assisted resin infusion/VARI. Measure how resin intrusion modifies aerogel pore structure and thermal response.
7. **Baseline and controls.** Prepare normal CFRP plus carbon-domain-only, BN-domain-only and combined constructions at comparable dimensions, layup and test conditions; the exact specimen count is not yet user-confirmed.
8. **Test properties.** Measure directional/effective conductivity k_x, k_y, k_z or in-plane vs through-thickness; temperature fields via IR thermography; solar spectral reflectance; mid-IR emittance where the equipment permits; mechanical properties (tensile/flexural/interlaminar strength or short-beam); mass/density.
9. **Run three thermal load cases.** Test external illumination alone, internal heater alone, and combined illumination + internal heater; measure peak and steady-state temperatures, spatial gradients, heat spreading and time to steady state under repeatable boundary conditions.
10. **Model structure-to-property relationship.** Use analytical thermal resistance baseline plus 2D/3D FEA (ANSYS/Python) to sweep pore alignment, porosity, density, BN/C ratio and laminate thickness; compare k tensor components, equivalent k_eff, heat flux and T fields to experiment. Treat porous-aerogel radiation as an effective heat-transfer component only with stated assumptions.
11. **Analyse processing–structure–property.** Fix three top-level headings: 1 Processing/Control Parameters → 2 Resulting Structure → 3 Resulting Properties. Draw 1→3 as experiment and 2→3 as simulation, as specified by user; identify missing factors from further reading.
12. **Optimize and validate.** Compare combined sample against baseline on external heat blocking, internal heat spreading, structural integrity and added weight; identify trade-offs and uncertainties before selecting a preferred architecture.

### Processing–Structure–Property (PSP) graph: three mandatory top-level headings

| Category | User-defined/target variables | Research/open items |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Processing / Control Parameters** | BN/C ratio, freezing temperature, freezing duration | Filler identity, concentration/solid loading, freezing direction, cooling rate, dispersing conditions, binders, drying and resin infusion |
| **2. Resulting Structure** | Shape (potentially honeycomb/hexagonal), density | Porosity, pore size, wall thickness, orientation/alignment, pore connectivity, anisotropy, defects |
| **3. Resulting Properties** | Mechanical stress/strength, heat transfer, optical reflectance | Directional conductivity, thermal resistance, emissivity, solar-weighted reflectance, internal/external thermal performance, interlaminar integrity |

- **1→3: Experiment**, not simulation.
- **2→3: Simulation**, not experiment.
- **1→2: Fabrication/process–morphology relationship**, assessed through process study and physical characterization.
- Keep exactly three named categories; add/update only the items beneath them.
